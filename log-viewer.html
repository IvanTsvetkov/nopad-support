NoPad opens multi-gigabyte log files on Mac by mapping them into memory rather than loading them, so a file larger than your memory can still open. Scratch a query beside it, search it, and compare it with yesterday's.

What it does with logs

  • Opens very large files. NoPad reads what it needs from the file instead of loading all of it.
  • Keeps the bytes as they are. Invalid bytes in a log are preserved rather than replaced.
  • Searches across files. Search one file or every open buffer.
  • Compares two logs. A split diff of two buffers, or a buffer against the clipboard.
  • Keeps a scratchpad beside it. Split panes, multiple carets and buffers that are kept between sessions, with nothing to name or save.

Private by design

Logs carry things that should not be pasted into a website. NoPad processes text on your device, with no account, no analytics and no tracking. Mapped files stay on the Mac they were opened on and are never synced.

Questions

Is NoPad free?

Yes. NoPad is free on the App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases.

Does it work on iPad or iPhone?

The large-file log viewer is a Mac feature. iPad edits and previews buffers up to 4 MB, and iPhone reads buffers synced from your other devices.

Which Mac do I need?

NoPad requires macOS 26 or later.
