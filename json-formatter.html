NoPad formats and inspects JSON without an account, a file name or a browser tab. Paste a response and it is detected by its content, laid out, and kept for you between sessions. All of it runs on your device.

What it does with JSON

  • Formats on paste. Paste JSON and NoPad lays it out. Key order stays as you wrote it. Formatting is one edit you can undo.
  • Points at the error. Malformed JSON is reported with its position, and your text stays as it was.
  • Shows a collapsible tree. Inspect a large response as a tree on Mac, and copy a value from it.
  • Minifies and sorts keys. Minify JSON and Sort JSON Keys are commands on Mac. Copy As gives you formatted or minified output without changing the buffer.
  • Compares two versions. Put two responses side by side in a split diff, or compare a buffer with the clipboard.

On Mac, iPad and iPhone

Mac and iPad both format and edit. iPhone is a reader: it opens buffers synced from your other devices, and you can copy or share them. Sync is optional, off by default, and uses your own private iCloud storage.

Why use an app instead of a website

An online formatter sends your text to someone else's server, and API responses are often full of things that should not leave your machine. NoPad processes text on your device. It has no account, no analytics and no tracking, and it keeps working offline.

Questions

Is NoPad free?

Yes. NoPad is free on the App Store, with no subscription and no in-app purchases.

Does NoPad send my JSON anywhere?

No. Formatting runs on your device. If you turn on iCloud sync, eligible buffers go to your own private CloudKit database so your own devices can reach them.

How large a JSON file can it format?

NoPad reads multi-gigabyte files on Mac by mapping them rather than loading them, but formatting works on the whole text, so very large files open for reading rather than reformatting.
